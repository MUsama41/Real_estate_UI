How Inheritance (Wirasat) Works for Property in Pakistan

October 13, 2026 11 min read

When a property owner dies in Pakistan, the property is not simply divided only between family members by a verbal family choice. First, the family must identify the property and confirm the legal heirs, determine each heir's share, collect the necessary documents, and update the property records. Inheritance (Wirasat) for property in Pakistan can involve some legal or official steps.

Having a right to inherit property and changing the property records are two different steps. The exact procedure may depend on the deceased's religion or family situation, the type of property, the province, available documents, and whether there is a dispute.

What Happens to Property After Someone Dies?

When a property owner dies, the family first needs to identify which property belonged to the deceased and who the legal heirs are.

In property inheritance in Pakistan, the procedure typically follows these steps:

Death → find out the property → classify the legal heirs → check the inheritance rules → find every heir's share → prepare the documents → update property records → divide, keep, or sell the property.

The process can be harder when the property is jointly owned, some property was previously transferred, there are multiple properties, or the ownership papers are missing. It's important to recognize that inheritance rights and the transfer of property records are not the same step. First, the heirs' rights are decided. Later, the property records can be updated.

Who Can Inherit Property in Pakistan?

Who can inherit property depends on the family situation and the applicable law. For Muslim families, wirasat property is typically divided according to Islamic inheritance rules and Pakistani law. The heirs may include a husband or wife, children, parents, siblings, or other close family members. But each family member does not automatically get a share. The share of one person can also depend on which other heirs are alive.

Before dividing the property, the family should identify all legal heirs and review the family records. They should also check whether there is a valid will, if any heir is a minor, if an heir died earlier, if the deceased had unpaid debts, or whether the property records have been updated.

How Are Inheritance Shares Calculated?

There is no fixed percentage just for each family. An inheritance share depends on the family members who are alive or the legal rules that apply. For example: imagine a person dies and leaves a spouse, children and a parent. We can't correctly divide the property until we know all the surviving heirs.

In Muslim families, wirasat or faraid rules can give different shares centered on which family members are alive. Families should not use a general percentage or online calculator without checking their full family situation. If the family situation or property papers are complex, then a qualified legal professional can help out only to calculate the right shares or clarify the next steps.

Why You Should Identify All Legal Heirs Before Transferring the Property

An important step in the inheritance of property is finding out who all the legal heirs are before changing the property records. Families may think that "Everybody knows who the heirs are," but this can cause complications later. An heir may be living abroad, a minor child may be missed, or some key documents may not be available. An heir who died earlier may also have children or other heirs who want to be considered.

Before dividing, transferring, or selling the property, check out all family and heir facts such as:

  • Heirs living in another city or country
  • Minor children who may have a share
  • Missing or incomplete documents
  • Family members who disagree about the heirs
  • An heir who died before the property was transferred
  • Property that has been inherited through some generations
  • Finding these complications in time can help avoid disputes and delays. It can also make the transfer procedure easier or help protect the rights of all legal heirs.

What Documents Are Usually Needed?

The documents required for property inheritance in Pakistan may be different depending on the property, province, local authority or family situation. The common documents may include:

Death certificate: Shows that the property owner has died.

CNIC or identity documents: Help to confirm the identity of the deceased or the heirs.

Proof of relationship: It shows that how the heirs are related to the deceased.

Property ownership documents: Show that the property belonged to the deceased.

Fard or land record: May be used to check out the property facts.

Mutation or intiqal records: Show changes made to the property record.

Succession documents: Help confirm the legal heirs.

Court orders: May be required if a court has given an order about the property.

Will or testamentary documents: May be required if the deceased left a legally valid will.

These documents support and confirm the deceased person, the legal heirs or the property. Depending on the property and jurisdiction, additional documents may be required. Families should check the current requirements with the relevant authority before starting the transfer process.

How Is Inherited Property Transferred Into the Heirs' Names?

After the legal heirs and their shares are confirmed, the property records may need to be changed to show the heirs' names. For land listed in government revenue records, this may involve inheritance mutation, also called intiqal. This means updating the land record after the owner's death or adding the rightful heirs' names.

Deceased owner → identify heirs → complete inheritance documents → apply for the transfer → update the property or land record → heirs' names are added to the record.

The exact steps can differ by province and by property type. Families should follow the current process for their property. If there is a dispute about ownership, shares, fraud or possession, they may want separate legal help.

What Is Mutation (Intiqal) in Inheritance?

Property mutation means updating the official land record when property is transferred to somebody else. In inheritance cases, inheritance mutation, also called intiqal, updates the land record after the property owner dies and records the names of the legal heirs. This keeps the property record up to date and helps heirs deal with the property later.

But mutation is not the same as actual ownership. It mainly updates the land record and does not automatically resolve disputes about who owns the property or what share each heir should receive. If the heirs dispute ownership or their inheritance shares, they may need to use a separate legal procedure to resolve it.

What Happens If the Heirs Disagree?

A property dispute between heirs can happen when family members don't agree about the property. For example, one heir may refuse to cooperate, demand a larger share, question the list of heirs, or take control of the property. They may also disagree about the property documents, refuse to sign the division papers, or not agree to sell the property.

The following step depends on the problem. It may involve a legal notice, a revenue office procedure, a civil case, a succession procedure, a partition case, or another legal option. If there is a dispute over ownership, possession, documents, or inheritance shares, legal advice can help you determine the right way to proceed.

Can an Heir Sell Inherited Property?

An heir may have a legal share in inherited property, but this doesn't necessarily mean they own a separate piece of land. For example, several heirs may own the same property together without dividing it into separate parts.

An heir may be able to sell their share, but the process can change when the property is still jointly owned. Before selling, the heirs should check the ownership records, inheritance documents, and their legal shares.

The directions can differ depending on the property and applicable law. Therefore, an heir should confirm their rights before selling.

What Happens If the Deceased Owned Property Jointly?

Before dividing property among heirs, first check how much of the property really belonged to the deceased. For example: if a house was jointly owned by two people and one owner died, the full house does not automatically become part of the deceased's property.

Only the deceased's ownership share is considered first. That share is then divided according to the applicable inheritance rules. This is why it is key to check the registry or title documents, land records, and other ownership papers just before calculating an inheritance share.

Special Situations That Can Complicate Wirasat

Some situations can make the inheritance process more complex and may need additional care.

Minor heirs: A child under 18 may just have a share in the inheritance. But additional legal steps may be required to protect the child's share.

Overseas heirs: An heir living outside Pakistan can still have inheritance rights. But their location may make documents, authorization, and participation more difficult.

Missing heirs: If an heir can't be found, then the family should not simply leave that person out of the inheritance. Their rights should be correctly considered.

Multiple inheritances: Sometimes, a property owner dies, or an heir dies later. In this case, the family may need to handle more than one inheritance procedure.

Incomplete records: Older properties may have missing documents, outdated mutations, and inconsistent information across different records. These problems may need to be checked and corrected.

Mortgage or other charges: A property may have a mortgage, loan, and other legal claim against it. Inheritance doesn't automatically remove these debts or obligations.

Common Mistakes Families Make During Property Inheritance

Relying on verbal agreements: A family agreement may not be enough to protect every heir or update official records.

Ignoring an overseas heir: An heir living abroad still has inheritance rights and should be included.

Dividing property too early: The family should first confirm how much property belonged to the deceased.

Treating mutation as the final step: Intiqal updates land records but may not solve each ownership dispute.

Using simple online calculations: Inheritance shares depend on the complete list of legal heirs.

Delaying record updates: Late updates can cause complications later.

Selling too soon: Property should be properly documented before heirs sell it.

A Practical Wirasat Checklist for Property Heirs

A simple checklist can help families move through the wirasat process step by step:

Step 1: Get the deceased person's death certificate.

Step 2: Make a list of all living legal heirs.

Step 3: Collect all property papers and ownership documents.

Step 4: Check which property or share was owned by the deceased.

Step 5: Determine which inheritance rules apply to the family.

Step 6: Work out the legal share of every heir.

Step 7: Complete the required inheritance and succession documents.

Step 8: Update the property or land records with the heirs' names.

Step 9: Choose whether to keep, divide, or sell the inherited property.

Step 10: Get legal assistance if there is a dispute, a missing heir, or a complex ownership problem.

Following these steps can help families avoid errors and make decisions later by checking the important facts.

Final Takeaway

To summarize, inheritance (Wirasat) for property in Pakistan isn't just about dividing property. First, the family should identify what property the deceased owned and identify all legal heirs. They should also check the inheritance rules and complete the required documents and property records. Later, the heirs can decide whether to keep, divide or sell the property. If there are minor or overseas heirs, joint property, missing documents or family arguments, legal guidance that can help out you avoid complications and delays.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is property inherited in Pakistan?

It depends on the law, family, or property ownership. The heirs are identified, shares are decided, documents are completed, and property records are updated.

Who are the legal heirs?

This depends on the family and applicable law. Spouses or children, parents, and other relatives may have rights but not every relative gets a share.

How are wirasat shares calculated?

Shares depend on the surviving heirs and the applicable inheritance directions. There is no fixed percentage for each family.

What is inheritance mutation (intiqal)?

It updates land or property records after the owner's death. It doesn't settle ownership disputes each time.

What documents are needed?

These may include the death certificate or ID documents, evidence of relationship, ownership papers, and land records.

Can one heir sell inherited property alone?

It depends on the heir's legal share and the type of ownership. A separate portion and an undivided share are different.

What if heirs disagree?

The solution depends on the dispute. It may involve legal notices or revenue offices, partition or court proceedings.

What happens to jointly owned property?

Only the deceased person's ownership share is considered for inheritance. The full property doesn't automatically become part of the estate.

Can an overseas Pakistani inherit property?

Yes, they may have inheritance rights. But they may need extra documents or legal steps if they live outside Pakistan.
